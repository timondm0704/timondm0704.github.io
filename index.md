## About Me

Hi, I'm Mao, a Research Data Analyst at Northeastern University's Bouvé College of Health Sciences (Roux Institute), where I analyze genetic and clinical data. I'm also pursuing a PhD in Northeastern's Industry Interdisciplinary PhD program, with research focused on variance heterogeneity, parent-of-origin effects, and pharmacogenetics.

My path has crossed continents and disciplines. After earning a bachelor's degree in GIS in China, I spent 11 years at an electric utility analyzing power supply data. Wanting to go deeper into analytics, I came to the US for a master's in Applied Data Analytics at Boston University, then moved into biomedical research, first at MaineHealth and, since 2023, at the Roux Institute. Across geography, energy, and healthcare, in both industry and academia, one thing hasn't changed: I'm a data person at heart, and I love uncovering the stories hidden in large, complex datasets.

I'm organized and comfortable managing several projects at once, and I enjoy stepping into new fields and learning what they need.

### Highlights

**Genome-wide analysis (GWAS):** built a GWAS pipeline on an HPC cluster to test gene-by-treatment interactions in a multi-site randomized controlled trial, processing hundreds of millions of variants with PLINK and REGENIE.<br>
**Statistical genetics research:** lead a study of parent-of-origin effects at the FTO obesity locus using Framingham Heart Study data, integrating mean, variance, and gene expression analyses.<br>
**Statistical method evaluation:** contributed the simulation study behind BifurcatoR, an R package and Shiny app for detecting variance heterogeneity.<br>
**11 years of large-scale data work:** managed smart meter data for over 2 million customers and led line loss analysis across 2,000+ power supply areas and 52 substations.
